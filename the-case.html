01

Where this starts

Before anything technical, one question.

Not the IT roadmap. The business. Every conversation we have starts here, because everything that follows has to point back at the answer.

If one thing worked properly over the next twelve months, what would make the biggest difference to you?

Worth writing down. We do, in front of you.

Most technology spend never gets traced back to a sentence like that. If a piece of work cannot be tied to one, it probably should not be funded.

02

The problem we solve

Three things have to happen. Only two of them have an owner.

Most businesses your size end up well covered on the first two, and quietly exposed on the third.

Something breaks
Your helpdesk or provider fixes it, usually well
Covered
Something needs building
A project team or a vendor builds it, to the brief they were given
Covered
Something needs deciding
Whether it is worth doing at all, and what comes next
Nobody

That third one lands on you, on top of running the business. So it gets made late, made twice, or made by whoever happens to be in the room. That is the gap, and it does not close on its own.

03

Why the current model stalls

The managed services model is not broken. It was never built to do this part.

Your provider is probably good at what they sell. The trouble is that what they sell was designed around tickets and uptime, and the thing you are missing sits above both.

01

The contract buys availability, not progress

Read the agreement you have. It commits to response times, uptime, and a monthly fee. There is not a line in it that says the business should be further ahead in twelve months than it is today.

02

The margin sits inside the stack

When the people advising you on the tools are the same people reselling the tools, the advice is never fully free. That is not dishonesty. It is just not independence, and you are paying for independence.

03

Volume is the business model

Profitability depends on serving hundreds of clients from one pool of people. Attention goes to whoever escalates loudest. Nobody in that pool is sitting with your numbers, thinking about your next three years.

04

Nothing ever gets switched off

A service that only ever adds is a service that only ever grows. No one in the arrangement is paid to tell you a project is not worth doing, or that a renewal should be challenged rather than processed.

What you are buying What is actually missing
Tickets closed › Decisions made, and owned
Uptime reported › Progress reported, to the board
Renewals processed › Renewals challenged, on your behalf
Requests fulfilled › The right work selected, the rest killed

You bought a service. What you needed was someone accountable for the direction. Those are different purchases.

Secure, at every stage
Stage 01

Stage 02

Stabilise

The tools question

People always want this answered early, so here it is, once. Five names, one line each.

MicrosoftThe platform you already own, and mostly underuse.

None of that is the point. It is the floor. It exists so the ground stops moving under you, and so everything built afterwards has something solid to sit on.

Most providers sell you those five names as the destination and bill you monthly for owning them. We treat them as the floor. Once the floor is solid and boring, you stop spending attention on tools and start compounding real gains on top of them.
