# myCIO: brief for an AI site builder

Written for the Wix Studio AI builder, but usable with any tool that takes a long description. It restates the positioning in `CLAUDE.md` and the live site; if those change, change this.

**Business name:** myCIO. Website mycio.co.nz. Auckland, New Zealand, working nationwide.

**What myCIO is.** myCIO is a senior technology leadership team for New Zealand organisations. We provide fractional CIO leadership, technology and digital transformation, AI strategy and delivery, and cyber security. We are the senior technology leadership an organisation would hire if it could justify a full-time executive team, available for the share of the week it actually needs. We are founder-led by Elton McDowall and Michael Nicolle, with a senior team behind them. The founders are in the room on every engagement. There are no account managers, no junior handoffs and no offshore desk. We are New Zealand owned and independent: we do not resell technology, so our advice is never shaped by what we sell.

**The problem we solve.** Three jobs happen in every business's technology. Something breaks, and a provider or an internal team fixes it. Something needs building, and a vendor builds it to the brief. Something needs deciding: whether it is worth doing at all, what it has to achieve, in what order, and what comes next. The first two jobs are usually well covered. The third seat is usually empty, or it falls to a CFO, owner or IT manager who is guessing. That empty seat is where money leaks and ambition stalls: renewals nobody challenges, cloud spend that grows without a return, security decided by whoever pitched last, and AI discussed every quarter and never shipped. myCIO takes that seat. Senior enough to set the direction, hands-on enough to deliver it, accountable for the outcome.

**Who we work with.** Two kinds of leader. The first has a clear plan for where the business is going and more on the roadmap than the team can carry; they need senior capacity beside them. The second knows something has to change and needs help putting the ambition into words, outcomes a board can fund, and a plan with an owner. Our clients are IT managers, CIOs, CFOs, COOs, general managers, owners and board members at New Zealand organisations that have outgrown ad hoc IT support or a managed services arrangement but cannot justify a full-time technology executive. Sectors include food processing, medical, retail, pharmaceutical and manufacturing. We treat in-house IT leaders as peers we add capacity to, never as people we replace.

**The nameable difference.** Everything stays in the client's name: systems, data, every licence, every contract, the documentation, the standards and the roadmap. We own the decisions and the change, not the tickets. Licensing goes through at cost with a margin the client can see. There is no lock-in, not even to us: a client can walk away at any time and keep the lot.

**The four responsibilities (keep this order).** One team, accountable for all of it.

1. Fractional CIO: owns the direction. Strategy, a rolling roadmap reviewed quarterly, governance and risk, vendor accountability, budget and board reporting in plain language, and the major decisions reviewed before they are signed.
2. Technology transformation: builds the foundations. Identity and access (Microsoft Entra, zero trust), cloud (Azure and Microsoft 365, workload placement, cost control), infrastructure and networks, modern work, stalled programmes picked up and finished, one documented standard.
3. AI strategy and delivery: puts the foundations to work. An AI strategy the board can fund: where AI pays off in the organisation and where it does not, policy and governance, data readiness, then the first working builds (assistants, automations, agents) made with the client's team in their own tenant and measured against the number they were meant to move.
4. Cyber security: the condition every piece of work meets, not a stage you finish. Posture against NIST CSF and SMB1001, governance and board reporting, managed detection and response, backups that work, a rehearsed incident plan, and the evidence insurers and customers ask for.

**How we work.** Every engagement starts with a thirty-minute call with one of the founders, no slide deck. The first question is about the business, not the IT: if one thing worked properly in the next twelve months, what would make the biggest difference? We will not quote a monthly number before we have seen the business. Then discovery on the floor, not from a questionnaire, and one visible win inside thirty days. Our method is The Climb: Discover, Stabilise, Standardise, Transform, with security wrapped around every stage. At the top, the work becomes a rhythm we call The Flywheel: strategy sets direction, transformation delivers it, operations keeps it turning. Three ways in: a fixed-price discovery and roadmap, a retained fractional CIO, or a single programme with an owner.

**Proof.** One client saved over $500,000 in a single competitive vendor review. Every piece of work carries a reason it was chosen and evidence of what it delivered. Sometimes our advice is not to spend the money.

**Tone of voice.** Plain, direct, senior, warm. Short sentences. No jargon, no buzzwords, no product names in headlines. We attack the managed-services model, never a specific provider. NZ English (organisation, standardise, licence). Never use em-dashes. Headlines often end on a second line in orange that lands the point. Example headline: "You know where the business needs to go. We get the technology there."

**Design direction.** Warm editorial, not a tech template. Off-white paper background (#f5f3ee), warm charcoal text (#202a30), one burnt orange accent (#cf581e) used sparingly: the logo slash, buttons, a rule across the top of columns, and the second line of headlines. Typeface Hanken Grotesk throughout; large headlines in a light weight. No cards, no drop shadows, no glows, no gradients, no icons. Content sits in columns with a thin orange rule above, lists as hairline rows. Generous white space. The logo is the word "my", an orange slash, then "CIO". Tagline: Strategy · Security · Technology.

**Imagery.** Real-looking documentary photography of work happening in New Zealand offices, boardrooms and factory floors: hands on a diagram, a team looking at a whiteboard, a security checklist on a desk. People seen from behind or in profile; faces only for the two founder portraits. No stock handshakes, no padlocks, no glowing circuit boards, no robots.

**Pages.** Home (the story: who it is for, the gap, the four responsibilities, how it runs, what you own, who turns up, proof, start with one thing). What we do, with one page per responsibility. How we work. Who we are. The journey (three situations a reader picks from, then what the first year looks like). Contact.

**Calls to action.** Primary everywhere: "Book your 30-minute call". Secondary: "Let's talk". Contact form fields: name, email, organisation, which situation describes you, and "what would make the biggest difference this year?" Email hello@mycio.co.nz. Footer line: Independent. In the room. NZ owned.

**What are the main goals of this site?**

One goal: a 30-minute call booked with a founder. Every page ends there, and the primary button everywhere reads "Book your 30-minute call". To earn that, the site does four things. A visitor recognises themselves in the first screen: stuck between security vendors, a cloud bill rising with no return, or AI that never ships. It reframes the problem: not a vendor, pricing or AI problem, but a decision nobody owns. It shows what happens next as a journey with a clock: the first call, day 30, day 90, year one. And it removes the risk of calling: a founder on the line, no slide deck, no monthly quote before discovery, and if the fit is wrong we say so. Secondary goals: the four responsibilities clear without reading as a menu; proof in one line with one number; Elton, Michael and the team made real; and a landing page for LinkedIn traffic that captures which situation each enquiry comes from. Not goals: selling products, ranking for tool names, or looking like a managed services provider.
