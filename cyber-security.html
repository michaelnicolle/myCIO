The moment people call

The questionnaire, the board question, or the near miss.

Security engagements start when someone outside IT asks a question IT cannot answer with evidence.

The insurer's questionnaire landed and nobody knows how to answer it honestly.

MFA everywhere? Backups tested? An incident plan? Ticking the box is easy. Having the evidence is the job.

A director asked what happens if we are breached.

Not whether. What happens. Who calls whom, what stops, how long, what it costs. The room went quiet.

Something nearly happened.

A payment nearly went to the wrong account. A login from somewhere nobody works. The near miss is the cheapest lesson you will ever get, if you act on it.

What we take ownership of

Posture, governance, response, assurance.

Security decisions are business decisions. That is why it sits with the leadership team, not in a separate contract.

01

Posture

An honest assessment against NIST CSF and SMB1001. Gaps included, not glossed over, with a prioritised plan to close them.

02

Governance

A live risk register, policies people can follow, and board reporting that shows the main risks, what is being done, and the improvement over time.

03

Detection and response

Managed detection and response with real people hunting threats, vulnerability management, and backups that are tested, not assumed.

04

Incident readiness

A response plan kept current and rehearsed. When something happens, we lead it. Your insurer sees the evidence they want.

05

Assurance

Insurer questionnaires, customer supplier assessments and audits answered with evidence rather than optimism.

06

Identity as the perimeter

Entra, conditional access and privileged access done properly, because in a cloud business identity is the control everything else depends on.
